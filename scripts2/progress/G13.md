## Decisions taken

- **Every verdict sentence carries the discrepancy *and* the tolerance.** A 47°
  discrepancy is a finding against ±3° and nothing at all against ±50°. A
  sentence with only the discrepancy is not quotable, and quotable is the whole
  point of this mode.

- **Three outcomes, not two.** `cannot-tell` is a real answer and is never
  rendered as a contradiction — an unmeasurable scene says nothing about the
  claim either way. There is a test asserting the word "inconsistent" does not
  appear in that case.

- **A partial measurement still gives a verdict.** If elevation is unbounded but
  azimuth is not, the azimuth alone can contradict a claim, and the sentence
  says which quantity it tested.

- **Both windows are always shown.** The morning-or-afternoon ambiguity is a
  real property of the problem, and silently collapsing it to one would be
  exactly the false confidence the app exists to avoid. The UI explains *why*
  there are two and what would resolve it, rather than just listing them.

- **No fitting window is an answer, not a failure.** When the sun never reaches
  the measured elevation at that place on that date, the app says so. Returning
  the nearest time would answer a question that has no answer.

- **Night is excluded before the fit is scored.** A sun below the horizon casts
  no shadow, so it cannot be the moment the photograph was taken however well
  the numbers line up.

- **Scoring is `grid.fitStrength`, shared with the global sweep.** A second
  scoring function would let the timeline and the globe disagree about the same
  instant.

- **Switching mode never discards the marking.** The geometry is identical in
  all three cases; only the unknown differs.

## Left for later slices

- G14's guards attach here: the low-sun refusal and the equinox and residual
  warnings must change what these modes show, not sit beside them as captions.
- G15's report reads `state.verdict`, `state.timeWindows` and `state.band`.
- The timeline is drawn at a fixed 900px and scaled by CSS. On a very wide
  window the hour labels get soft — G17 should judge whether that matters.
- `modes.timeWindows` sweeps at 2-minute steps, so a window edge is accurate to
  ±2 minutes. That is well inside the tolerance the bands imply, but the report
  should not quote window edges to the minute as if they were exact.
