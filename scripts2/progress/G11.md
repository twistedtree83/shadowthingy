# G11 — Map: orthographic globe, bundled coastlines, soft band

Issue: #16 · Branch: `sprint/gnomon-mvp` · Status: **shipped**

## What shipped

An orthographic globe over Natural Earth 110m land, draggable to rotate, with
the band rendered as a soft-edged raster and the claimed location marked so a
reader can see at a glance whether it falls inside. Plus `grid`'s dense strength
field and bilinear sampling, with 4 new assertions; 175 total.

**The gate, verified in the browser:** the fixture's claimed place (Sydney) comes
back at strength **0.957** inside its own band, London at **0**, with the
azimuth filter applied — 3,681 cells of 519,120. The marker sits visibly on the
band arc.

## Decisions taken

- **The band is a raster, not thousands of shapes.** Every screen pixel is
  inverse-projected and the strength field sampled bilinearly. Two reasons: an
  unbounded band covers 260k cells and would be unusable drawn cell by cell; and
  interpolation makes the edge genuinely soft, where per-cell rectangles would
  step at the grid resolution. **The softness has to come from the tolerance,
  not from how finely the world was diced.**

- **Band alpha is capped at 200/255.** A solid patch would hide the coastlines
  underneath, which are exactly the context that turns a coloured arc into
  "somewhere in the Tasman Sea".

- **The graticule is drawn after the band** so the grid stays legible through
  it; the coastlines are drawn before, so the band reads as lying on the globe.

- **Rotation is clamped at the poles.** Without it the globe can be tumbled into
  an upside-down state the user then has to fight their way out of.

- **A failed coastline fetch is named, not blank.** The globe still works
  without land — the band is the finding and the coastlines are orientation —
  so the caption says "coastlines unavailable offline" rather than showing an
  empty circle.

## Two rendering bugs, one nearly shipped

1. **`putImageData` overwrites; it does not composite.** Writing the band
   straight to the visible context erased the globe fill and every coastline
   beneath it and left transparent holes showing the page background. The result
   looked like a plausible empty ocean with a band floating on it — I spent a
   while checking whether the TopoJSON had loaded before realising the drawing
   was being deleted after the fact. The band now goes to an offscreen canvas
   and is composited with `drawImage`, which respects alpha.

2. **The coastlines were drawn at #242b32 on a #12161b globe** and were
   invisible even once they were being drawn at all. Land is now #333e49 with a
   #5d6b78 stroke. A map whose geography cannot be seen is decoration.

## The network posture, enforced

The map fetches one file — `world-atlas@2.0.2/land-110m.json`, 56 KB — at page
load, before any image exists, identically for every user.

**There is no tile server and that is the point rather than a limitation.**
Tiles are requested per-viewport *after* the analysis, so the sequence of
requests *is* the region of interest, handed to a third party as a side effect
of looking at the result. `npm run check:network` fails the build on a tile URL
template or a tile-based library, and has a test asserting it catches one even
on an otherwise-allowed host.

## Environment note

This environment's egress policy blocks `cdn.jsdelivr.net`, so the browser tests
route all five CDN URLs to locally-fetched copies of the real packages
(`scratchpad/serve.mjs`). Same libraries, same script tags, same code path.
Anywhere the CDN is reachable, no workaround is needed.
