# G4 — Synthetic scene generator and the vanishing-point round trip

Issue: #5 · Branch: `sprint/gnomon-mvp` · Status: **shipped**

## What shipped

`Gnomon.synth` — a 3D scene generator with known camera, sun and poles;
analytic shadow tips; projection to image coordinates; analytic vanishing
points; and three corruption hooks. 18 headless assertions.

The round trip runs across **six camera orientations**, including two tilted,
one rolled, and one tilted-and-rolled hard. All three vanishing points come back
matching the analytically-computed ones to 1e-6, with condition number 0 to
1e-7 — the lines really do concur.

## The finding that matters: a uniform ground tilt is invisible, and correctly so

**G8's issue says to tilt the ground plane 10° and assert the residual rises.
That will not happen, and the reason is worth understanding before G8 starts.**

Take a gravity-vertical pole of height `h` on a plane with normal `n`. The
shadow tip solves `n·(top − t·sun − base) = 0`, giving `t = h(n·up)/(n·sun)` —
so `t ∝ h`, and:

- **shadow** = `tip − base` = `h·[up − k·sun]` where `k = (n·up)/(n·sun)`
- **ray** = `tip − top` = `−t·sun` ∝ `sun`

Both are the *same direction for every pole* regardless of height or position,
so all three families stay internally parallel and every vanishing point stays
perfectly conditioned. And the coplanarity determinant is identically zero,
because `shadow` is by construction a combination of `up` and `sun`:

```
det[up, up − k·sun, −sun] = −k·det[up, sun, sun] = 0
```

This is not a gap in the residual — it is the residual being right. Elevation is
`90° − angle(d_up, d_sun)`, and on a uniformly tilted ground **both of those
directions are still correct**, so the answer is still correct. There is nothing
to warn about.

There is a genuine blind spot next door, and it should be documented rather than
tested for: if the marked objects are perpendicular to the *slope* rather than
to gravity — fence posts on a hillside — then `d_up` is the plane normal, the
recovered elevation is measured from the wrong horizon, and the residual is
still zero. No purely projective check can catch that. It belongs in G14's
warnings and G17's assumptions list, not in a test.

The test `"a uniform ground tilt leaves every family internally consistent"`
asserts this explicitly so nobody later "fixes" it.

## What does break coplanarity, with a hook each

| Hook | What it models | Family it degrades |
|---|---|---|
| `pole.lean = {tilt, bearing}` | a leaning object marked as vertical | vertical |
| `pole.groundNormal` | a differently-sloped patch under one object | shadow |
| `pole.sun` | an object composited in from a differently-lit photo | ray |

Each has a test asserting the corresponding condition number rises above the
clean case. These are what G8's negative cases should use.

## Decisions taken

- **World frame is East-North-Up, camera frame is the usual pinhole.** Azimuth
  is from north clockwise, matching the solar engine's convention, so G8 can
  compare recovered azimuth against G2 without a frame conversion sitting
  between them waiting to hide a sign error.

- **Pitch rotates about the camera's own right axis, not about world east.**
  The two are identical at yaw 0 and diverge everywhere else — the level and
  yawed orientations would both have passed either way, which is exactly why the
  tilted-and-rolled case is in the list.

- **`project` throws for points behind the camera** rather than returning the
  mirrored coordinates that a naive divide produces. A point behind the camera
  cannot be marked, so a scene containing one is a broken fixture and should say
  so.

- **`synth.families()` is shared with the shell.** Marks and synthetic scenes
  travel the identical path into `geom.vanishingPoint`, so the round trip tests
  the real code path rather than a parallel one.

## Snags

Two fixture bugs, both worth recording because they look like geometry bugs:

1. **A camera fixed at one position cannot see the field from every
   orientation.** At yaw −63° the shadows fell behind it. Fixed by setting the
   camera back along its own bearing.

2. **Backing off along the full view direction put the camera at z = 0 whenever
   pitch was 0** — and a camera sitting exactly in the ground plane projects the
   entire ground to a single horizon line, so every base and tip became
   collinear. Fixed by making the setback horizontal and the height fixed and
   independent. The failure presented as "level camera fails, hard tilt passes",
   which is the opposite of what a real geometry bug looks like.

## Note for whoever picks up G7

`synth.scene(spec).truth` carries `focal`, `principal`, `elevation`, `azimuth`,
`sunVector`, `upDirection`, `shadowDirection`, `rayDirection` and the three
analytic `vanishing` points. Compare vanishing points with the sign-agnostic
`sameDirection` helper — homogeneous vectors are defined only up to scale and
sign.

Tier 4 calibration needs `v_z` and `v_shadow` to be genuinely orthogonal
directions. They are, in every scene here, so a Tier 4 failure against these
fixtures is a real bug rather than a degenerate input.
