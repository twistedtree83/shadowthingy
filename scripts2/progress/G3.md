# G3 — Homogeneous geometry kernel: vanishing points and conditioning

Issue: #4 · Branch: `sprint/gnomon-mvp` · Status: **shipped**

## What shipped

`Gnomon.geom` — cross-product line and intersection primitives, Hartley
normalisation, a cyclic Jacobi eigen-solver for symmetric 3×3, and the
least-squares vanishing point with a condition number. 16 headless assertions
plus a `projective geometry` suite that runs in the visible panel.

## Decisions taken

- **Being at infinity is not poor conditioning, and they are reported
  separately.** This was the most important call in the slice. A parallel family
  agrees *perfectly* about its direction — it just meets nowhere finite, and
  that direction is exactly what the sun recovery needs. Collapsing the two into
  one "bad" flag would throw away good data on the most common photograph there
  is (a level camera looking at vertical poles). So the result carries
  `atInfinity` and `wellConditioned` as independent facts, and the parallel test
  asserts `wellConditioned === true`.

- **`conditionNumber` is σ₃/σ₂, not σ₂/σ₃.** Both are "the ratio of the two
  smallest singular values" as the spec says, but this orientation *rises* with
  degeneracy: 0 when the lines concur exactly, → 1 as the null direction stops
  being separated. Higher is worse, which needs no remembering, and it matches
  the conventional sense of the phrase. The reciprocal would have read
  `Infinity` for a perfect two-line family, which is a confusing way to say
  "exact".

- **Hartley normalisation is load-bearing, not polish.** Line vectors built from
  raw pixel coordinates have a third component of order x·y — around 10⁶ on a
  large image — so after unit normalisation the first two components sit near
  10⁻⁶ and lose their precision. Normalising first keeps every component O(1),
  which is what makes the condition number mean anything at all. There is a
  scale-invariance test asserting the same scene at 1× and 8× agrees; it fails
  without this.

- **`magnitude` is exposed but no distance policy is applied.** `geom` does not
  know the image size, so it must not decide what counts as "too far out to
  mean anything". It reports how far the intersection landed and lets G7/G8
  apply their own scale.

- **A zero-length segment throws.** `cross(p, p)` is `[0,0,0]`, which would
  contribute nothing to `AᵀA` and silently reduce the effective line count. G6
  can produce these from a double-click, so failing loudly here is better than a
  vanishing point quietly computed from fewer lines than the user marked.

- **`Math.sign(0)` bug avoided explicitly.** In the Jacobi rotation, `theta` is
  0 exactly when the two diagonal entries are equal — the case that most needs
  rotating. `Math.sign(0)` is `0`, which gives `t = 0` and stalls the sweep. The
  code forces the positive branch. The hand-computed `[[2,1,0],[1,2,0],[0,0,5]]`
  case is exactly this situation and catches it.

## Gates added

- `npm run verify` green — 31 assertions, purity clean, network clean.
- **New: "every suite registered for the in-page panel is green."** This is the
  mechanism that stops the panel drifting from the suite. A slice can no longer
  ship a panel showing FAIL to a user while CI stays green.

## Left for later slices

- The `geom` debug section has nothing to show until G6 supplies real marks; the
  panel currently renders the `projective geometry` self-check suite instead.
- `CONDITION_LIMIT` is 0.05 and deliberately loose. It only has to catch
  families that are not a point at all — G9's Monte Carlo does the real
  quantifying. G17 tunes it against real images.

## Note for whoever picks up G4

`geom.vanishingPoint(segments)` takes `[{a: [x,y], b: [x,y]}, ...]` in pixel
coordinates and returns `{v, point, atInfinity, magnitude, conditionNumber,
wellConditioned, unbounded, singularValues}`. `v` is unit-norm homogeneous in
the *original* pixel frame — the Hartley transform is undone internally.

Compare vanishing points with a sign-agnostic parallel test: `v` is defined only
up to scale and sign, so `|v̂ · ê| ≈ 1` is the assertion, not componentwise
equality. `test/geom.test.js` has `sameDirection` doing exactly this — reuse the
shape.
