# G8 — Sun direction, sign resolution, and the validity residual

Issue: #9 · Branch: `sprint/gnomon-mvp` · Status: **shipped**

## What shipped

`Gnomon.sun` — direction recovery through K, sign resolution from the marks,
elevation, azimuth against a reference bearing, and the coplanarity residual as
the headline confidence figure. 27 headless assertions; 133 total.

## The gate

**Elevation and azimuth within 0.5° of ground truth across 16 combinations** —
four camera orientations (two tilted, two rolled, one each way) × four suns
spanning 15°–63° elevation and all four quadrants of azimuth. Measured error is
under 0.01°.

**Residual ≈ 0 for every valid scene**, and rising for each corruption:

| corruption | residual |
|---|---|
| clean | 0.00° |
| pole leaning 3° / 6° / 12° | rises monotonically, >1° by 12° |
| one object on a differently-sloped patch | > 0.5° |
| one object composited from a different sun | > 1° |

Browser-verified end to end on the fixture photograph: elevation **56.42°**
against a truth of 56.42°, azimuth **278.30°** against 278.30° — error 0.000° —
with residual 0.00° and a shadow multiplier of ×0.664.

That is a JPEG going in one end and the sun's position coming out the other,
agreeing with what the solar engine independently says the sun was doing at the
time and place the EXIF claims.

## The bug that cost the most: a single image line does not determine a 3D direction

The reference-direction handling first computed the marked line's *point at
infinity* and treated it as the line's vanishing point. That is only correct
when the line happens to lie parallel to the image plane, and it threw the
azimuth off by **38°** on a camera tilted 17°.

A single image line fixes only the plane through the camera centre that contains
it; the line could point anywhere within that plane. The reference direction is
recoverable at all only because it is **known to lie in the ground plane**, so
the answer is the intersection of two planes: the projecting plane (normal `Kᵀl`
in camera coordinates) and the ground plane (normal `d_up`). Their cross product
is the direction.

Elevation was exactly right throughout, which is what made this findable — the
failure was isolated to the one quantity that used the reference.

## Decisions taken

- **Sign resolution runs on image *displacement*, not "toward the vanishing
  point".** As `t` grows, the image of `A + t·d` approaches the vanishing point
  of `d` — but only when the point at infinity in that direction is in front of
  the camera. When `d` points behind (`dz < 0`) the image slides the *other*
  way. Taking "toward the vanishing point" as the rule would resolve the sign
  backwards for exactly the tilted cameras that are interesting. Each of the
  three signs is pinned to a displacement the user actually drew: base→top,
  base→tip, top→tip.

- **Sign resolution votes across every object.** One badly-placed mark cannot
  flip a direction, and a flipped direction is a 180° error rather than a small
  one.

- **`d_sun` points *toward* the sun; `d_ray` points away.** The spec's
  `α = 90° − angle(d_up, d_sun)` needs the direction of the light, while the
  sign is resolvable only from the ray the user drew (top → shadow tip), which
  runs the other way. Both are returned, named for what they are, rather than
  leaving a negation implicit somewhere and inviting a sign error later.

- **Azimuth is measured clockwise from north**, matching the solar engine, so
  G13 can compare a recovered azimuth with a predicted one without a conversion
  sitting between them waiting to hide a sign error. The cross-product term is
  counter-clockwise-positive, hence the negation in `bearingDelta`.

- **The residual colours itself.** Green below 1°, amber to 3°, red above. A
  rising residual means an assumption broke, so it stops looking like an
  ordinary reading once it does.

- **Azimuth-only is a mode, not an error.** When `v_sun` is poorly conditioned
  the elevation is the only casualty — it is the sole quantity depending on the
  ray direction. Azimuth comes from the shadow lying on the ground and survives,
  so the readout says "azimuth-only mode" rather than reporting a failure.

## The residual's independence, demonstrated

There is a test that solves the same scene with a focal length wrong by 35%. The
elevation moves by more than a degree — and the residual stays at zero, because
coplanarity is a property of the scene rather than of the camera model. That is
what makes the residual a real check rather than a restatement of the
calibration.

## Left for later slices

- G9 samples `focalPx` across `calibration.sweep` and perturbs the marks,
  re-running `sun.solve` 500 times.
- G13's Verify mode compares `sun.azimuth` against `solar.position().azimuth`.
- **The residual thresholds (1°, 3°) are guesses** and G14/G17 must tune them
  against real photographs.
- A known blind spot, from G4: objects perpendicular to a *slope* rather than to
  gravity give a wrong elevation with a zero residual. No projective check can
  catch it. It belongs in G14's warnings and G17's assumptions list.
