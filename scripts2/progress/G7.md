# G7 — Calibration cascade: five tiers, fall-through, and disagreement

Issue: #8 · Branch: `sprint/gnomon-mvp` · Status: **shipped**

## What shipped

`Gnomon.calib` — all five tiers, tried in order, with the winning tier setting
the sweep width every downstream band inherits. Disagreement detection, crop
penalty, and the readout wiring. 16 headless assertions; 106 total.

## The gate

**Tier 4 recovers the focal length to within 1% of ground truth** across 900,
1200, 1450, 1900 and 2600 px, on a tilted-and-rolled camera. Measured error is
under 0.01% — the limit is the solver, not the method.

Browser-verified end to end on four fixtures:

| fixture | tier | focal | error | band | disagreement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clean | 1 | 1350 px | 0.00% | ±2% | none |
| **stripped** | **4** | **1350 px** | **−0.00%** | **±15%** | none |
| cropped | 1 | 1350 px | 0.00% | ±8% | 1350 vs 969 (−28%) |
| faked | 1 | 2150 px | +59% | ±2% | 2150 vs 1350 (−37%) |

The stripped row is the one worth dwelling on: **six clicks and no metadata at
all recover the focal length exactly**, and the app says ±15% rather than
pretending to the ±2% it would claim with EXIF. That is the honesty rule
working, and it is visible in the band widths rather than in a caveat.

## Decisions taken

- **A resize needs no second rescaling.** Field of view is unchanged by a
  resize, so basing the calculation on the decoded width is already correct.
  Applying the resize ratio on top — which the spec's wording could be read as
  asking for — would be wrong by exactly that ratio. A crop is genuinely
  different: the decoded width spans only part of the recorded field of view, so
  the recorded width is the right basis there.

- **A crop multiplies the sweep by 4 rather than refusing.** Every tier depends
  on the principal point and a crop moves it somewhere unknown. Widening keeps
  an answer available while making the uncertainty visible, which is the honest
  trade; refusing would push users toward guessing elsewhere.

- **The disagreement threshold is 15%, matching Tier 4's own sweep.** A
  divergence smaller than the method's own looseness is not evidence of
  anything, and flagging it would train users to ignore the flag.

- **Disagreement never changes the chosen focal length.** The EXIF tier still
  wins the cascade; both numbers are reported side by side. Averaging would
  destroy exactly the signal that makes the divergence worth seeing — and a
  blended value would be wrong in a way neither input was.

- **Tier 4 falls through on a vanishing point at infinity, and that is not a
  failure.** A level camera sends verticals to infinity; the orthogonality
  constraint then has no equation left to solve. The note says so rather than
  reporting a poor answer, because "no constraint" and "weak constraint" are
  different things.

## Two bugs this slice caught in earlier work

1. **The fixture's EXIF contradicted its own geometry.** It claimed a 35mm
   equivalent while the synthetic camera was 1350 px on an 1800 px frame — 27mm.
   The app flagged a 23% divergence, correctly, on a fixture that was supposed
   to be clean. `fixture-scene.js` now *derives* the 35mm equivalent from the
   camera actually used. A tool that finds a real inconsistency in its own test
   data on the first run is behaving as advertised.

2. Prompted by that, `scene-faked.jpg` now exists: metadata that lies by 1.6×,
   so the disagreement path has a fixture that trips it **on purpose** rather
   than by accident.

## Left for later slices

- G8 consumes `state.calibration.focalPx` and `principalPoint` to build `K`.
- G9 consumes `sweep` — that is the whole reason it is computed here.
- Tier 3 works and is tested but the UI marks a rectangle without yet checking
  it looks rectangular. A wildly non-rectangular quad will produce a confident
  wrong answer at ±10%; G14 or G17 should add a plausibility check.
- The crop penalty of 4× is a guess. **G17 must tune it against real cropped
  images** — it is exactly the sort of threshold that cannot be set from a test.

## Note for whoever picks up G8

`calib.solve()` returns `principalPoint` and `focalPx`, which is everything `K`
needs. Check `principalPointTrustworthy` before believing the centre: on a
cropped image it is false, and the direction vectors inherit that error.

`state.calibration` on the shell side is refreshed by `recalibrate()`, wired to
`window.gnomonOnMarksChanged`.
